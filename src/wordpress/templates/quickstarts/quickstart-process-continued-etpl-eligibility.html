Process Continued ETPL Eligibility

Use this when an existing California ETPL program is approaching its continued-eligibility review and needs to remain active.

Before you act: This playbook translates current California and federal ETPL rules into an operating sequence. Official sources, the current CalJOBS workflow, and your adopted local policy control. Source check: October 7, 2026.
Use this when

A currently or previously eligible program is entering its renewal/reapplication cycle.

You need to decide

Whether the provider submission is complete, whether annual reporting is satisfied, which performance measures apply, and whether the program remains eligible.

Verify against

WSD25-02 continued-eligibility requirements, the current ETP reporting notice, CalJOBS expiration/reapplication information, and any separate adopted local policy.

Have these ready

Program expiration/reapplication status · provider/profile updates · current assurances · annual ETP reporting status · program costs/details · performance information · prior eligibility history · local policy.

60-second pathIdentify cycle → provider updates by State deadline → verify reporting → apply measure-by-measure performance rules → Local Board decision → nominate/submit → EDD decision → verify new eligibility period.

1. Start from the program's actual eligibility cycle

Continued eligibility is not an informal annual check. Use the program's current ETPL history and the State cycle that applies to it.

Standard California cycle: WSD25-02 uses March 31 for the provider's continued-eligibility submission, May 15 for the Local Board determination, and June 30 for the State decision. Verify the current directive and CalJOBS record before relying on those dates.

2. Confirm the provider updated what changed

  • Provider profile and contacts.
  • Current assurances and required documents.
  • Program cost, length, location, delivery, credential, curriculum, and occupational mapping.
  • Operating approval/accreditation/exemption evidence where applicable.

3. Verify annual ETP reporting separately

Annual ETP reporting and continued eligibility interact, but they are not the same task. Confirm that required All Student data were submitted for the applicable reporting cohort. A program that was on the ETPL for any part of the reporting program year can have reporting obligations even if it later closed or changed status.

4. Apply performance rules measure by measure

WSD25-02 contains continued-eligibility performance requirements and exemptions. Do not convert a missing denominator into zero performance. The amended directive clarifies that an exemption can apply to a measure when there is less than one complete program year of data or fewer than 10 students in that measure's denominator. Evaluate the actual measure, denominator, and available period.

Do not: apply a blanket “two years of data” rule unless the controlling State requirement or your separate lawful local policy actually says that for the decision you are making.

5. Keep California eligibility and local eligibility separate

The Local Board's California ETPL determination must use the State requirements. If your Local Board has separately adopted local ETPL standards, apply them as a second decision. A local rule cannot silently become a State criterion.

6. Treat relisting correctly

A program that was previously eligible and is seeking relisting generally follows the continued-eligibility pathway rather than being rewritten as a brand-new initial application. Preserve the eligibility history.

7. Record the decision trail

Document the cycle, evidence reviewed, performance measures/exemptions, Local Board determination, State submission, State decision, and new eligibility period.

Common mistakes

  • Missing the March/May/June cycle because staff wait for an email reminder.
  • Confusing annual reporting with the continued-eligibility decision.
  • Treating missing data as zero.
  • Applying local standards during the State eligibility determination.
  • Creating a new initial case for a previously eligible program and losing its history.

Stop and escalate when

  • The program's expiration or reapplication status is unclear in CalJOBS.
  • Required reporting is missing or disputed.
  • A performance exemption is unclear because denominator or cohort data are incomplete.
  • Operating authority has expired or materially changed.
  • The provider is facing removal and appeal rights may be triggered.
