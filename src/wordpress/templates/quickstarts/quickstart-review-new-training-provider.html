Review a New Training Provider

Use this when an organization that has never been approved through the California ETPL process wants one or more training programs considered for WIOA-funded Individual Training Accounts.

Before you act: This playbook translates current California and federal ETPL rules into an operating sequence. Official sources, the current CalJOBS workflow, and your adopted local policy control. Source check: October 7, 2026.
Use this when

A provider is new to the California ETPL, even if the organization already operates as a school, college, nonprofit, apprenticeship sponsor, or business.

You need to decide

Who owns the provider review, whether the provider meets the California eligibility requirements, which program reviews are also needed, and when the case is ready to nominate to EDD.

Verify against

WSD25-02, current CalJOBS ETPL procedures, operating-authority evidence, Section 188 assurances, and any separate lawful local ETPL standards.

Have these ready

Provider legal name and headquarters · California locations · provider type · operating approval/exemption/accreditation evidence as applicable · CalJOBS provider profile · CA ETP assurances · proposed programs · provider contact · local policy.

60-second pathRoute the case → review the provider → review each program separately → correct gaps → complete the Local Board California ETPL determination → nominate to EDD → track State decision → apply any separate local ETPL review.

1. Route the provider before reviewing it

Start with geography and provider type. Under California's role split, provider-profile responsibility generally follows the provider's headquarters, while program review generally follows the physical training location. Those can be different Local Boards.

Special route: If the provider has no California location and offers online training to California students, do not invent a local eligibility review. WSD25-02 routes that case to the State ETPL Coordinator.

2. Keep provider review and program review separate

Provider-level questionProgram-level question
Is this organization eligible to participate?Does this specific training program meet the California program requirements?
Does the provider have the required operating authority or qualifying pathway?Is the program, location, credential, cost, delivery format, and occupational connection accurately represented?
Are provider assurances and required profile information complete?Does the program meet applicable performance requirements or a valid new-program pathway?

A provider can have several programs. Approval of the provider does not automatically approve every program.

3. Verify the provider's authority to operate

Match the organization to the correct California pathway. For private postsecondary schools, this often means current BPPE Approval to Operate or a qualifying exemption. When an approval applies to a specific location or program, confirm the evidence at that level too. Higher education and other provider types can use different qualifying pathways.

Do not: treat a website, business license, accreditation logo, or provider statement as a substitute for the operating-authority evidence required by the applicable ETPL pathway.

4. Review the provider profile and assurances

  • Legal/provider identity matches the supporting evidence.
  • Headquarters and training locations are accurate.
  • Required provider contacts are current.
  • The current CA ETP assurances are completed and stored in the required CalJOBS location.
  • The provider understands annual ETP performance reporting obligations.
  • No unresolved fact suggests the provider has lost required operating authority or materially misrepresented information.

5. Review every proposed program

Each program needs its own review. Confirm the program name, curriculum/credential, location, delivery format, total cost, length, CIP/occupational connection, and applicable performance information. A newly created program without historical performance data is not automatically ineligible; use the new-program pathway in WSD25-02 instead of fabricating data or failing it solely for having no history.

6. Use the CalJOBS handoff as a controlled checkpoint

  1. Provider profile exists and required documents are uploaded.
  2. Program records are created and submitted.
  3. Local review is complete and documented.
  4. Any provider/program corrections are resolved.
  5. Only then nominate the eligible provider/program record to EDD.
  6. Track the EDD review separately from the Local Board determination.
Timing: WSD25-02 establishes a 30-day Local Board review period for a complete initial application and a subsequent State review period. Use the actual received/submission dates and the current directive rather than an internal target date.

7. Document the determination

Your file should make it easy for another coordinator to answer: What was reviewed? Which evidence supported each requirement? What was missing and corrected? When did the Local Board decide? When was the case nominated to EDD? What did EDD decide?

Common mistakes

  • Reviewing the school but not the specific program.
  • Using local preferences to deny the California ETPL determination.
  • Assuming accreditation automatically satisfies the provider's operating-authority pathway.
  • Starting a brand-new provider review when only a new program is being added.
  • Promising WIOA payment because the provider is approved. Participant training approval is a separate decision.

Stop and escalate when

  • The correct reviewer cannot be determined.
  • Operating authority is expired, inconsistent, or disputed.
  • The provider has no California location and is online-only.
  • There is evidence of intentional misrepresentation, fraud, or substantial noncompliance.
  • The Local Board wants to add a State eligibility criterion not found in WSD25-02.
