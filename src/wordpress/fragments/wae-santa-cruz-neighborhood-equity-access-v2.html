This is not WIOA performance data. The tool separates neighborhood conditions, community composition, access assets, and interpretation so each type of evidence can inform a decision without being mistaken for a score or conclusion.

Read the map carefully: darker shading means a larger value for the selected layer. It does not mean a neighborhood is better, worse, more deserving, or ranked. Race and ethnicity are shown only as descriptive community composition. American Community Survey condition measures show the 90% margin of error when available.

No published estimate

Each color scale shows the range among Santa Cruz County census tracts for the selected condition. Hatched tracts have no published estimate and are not treated as 0. Click or keyboard-select a tract to see its value.

Coverage standard

Strong coverage: at least 90% of tracts have a value. Partial: 75–89%. Limited: below 75%. During the Santa Cruz pilot, conditions with at least one usable published value remain selectable and their exact coverage is disclosed. A condition with no usable tract data is disabled.

About the map data

Sources: OEHHA / CalEPA CalEnviroScreen 5.0; U.S. Census Bureau 2020–2024 American Community Survey 5-year Data Profiles and Detailed Tables; Santa Cruz County GIS; Santa Cruz County's Strategic Plan: 2020 Census tract-to-neighborhood outreach geography; and official access-point sources from Workforce Santa Cruz County / California EDD, Goodwill Central Coast, Cabrillo College, WASC Adult Education, Santa Cruz County Office of Education, Santa Cruz and Watsonville public libraries, and Santa Cruz METRO. ACS condition measures are read only from the governed static tract file and must pass the same coverage gate before publication. Community composition currently uses CalEnviroScreen 5.0 published race/ethnicity tract fields; a direct 2020–2024 ACS precision replacement is staged behind its own validation gate. Race and ethnicity are descriptive context only and are not used as a need score, ranking, Decision Lens input, or resource-allocation rule. Access assets are service-zone context, not a distance or travel-time measure. Neighborhood labels are descriptive and should not be read as formal boundaries. Hatched tracts represent missing estimates and are not zero values.
