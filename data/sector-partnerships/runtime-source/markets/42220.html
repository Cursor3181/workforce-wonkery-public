What it means

Use the partnership only where collective action adds value.

Sonoma agriculture has a large economic footprint but is also restructuring. The county's latest crop report shows falling winegrape tonnage and value, while the Farm Bureau already provides producer governance and training capacity. The missing layer is a workforce agenda that goes beyond compliance toward resilience and advancement.

Worker value

What workers should gain

Sonoma County's 2026 single-adult living wage is $31.30/hour. Industrial maintenance and agricultural management clear it, while common equipment and frontline work may not. Worker Value therefore depends on technical progression and resilience during restructuring.

The move

Build a workforce action portfolio inside Sonoma County Farm Bureau rather than create a second broad agriculture partnership.

Local operating story

The restructuring pressure is visible in the crop data.

Sonoma County reports that significant grape tonnage was left on vines in 2025 because of decreased demand; winegrape value fell 7.46 percent and harvested tonnage fell 11.66 percent. Farm Bureau already delivers 61 safety and compliance courses plus custom onsite employer training. The workforce opportunity is to build on that platform while moving beyond compliance into portable technical skills and advancement for workers affected by change.

The story

Sonoma already has a healthcare pathway alliance. The next step is to connect activity to advancement.

CTE Foundation reports more than $316,000 invested in healthcare workforce development in 2025-26, support for 30 students in a CNA certification program, and four healthcare forums with employers and educators.

It also founded and now leads the North Bay Health Pathways Alliance. The region therefore does not need another healthcare table. It needs employer-owned pathway actions that show experience-to-hire conversion, credential or licensure progression, wage gains and retention.

Building trades & construction

Suggested geography: Sonoma County / North Bay construction labor market

Why it surfaced: Sonoma has strong construction workforce infrastructure through North Bay Building Trades, registered apprenticeship, prevailing-wage pathways and the County's Community Workforce Agreement. Those systems already provide worker progression, benefits and labor-management coordination.

What the review found: The existing system is strong enough that a new partnership should not be created by default. Project labor agreements show collective action on covered projects, but they do not establish a standing cross-contractor workforce problem that needs another table.

Reopen when: Contractors and trades identify a shared capacity, apprenticeship-completion, access or specialty-trade problem that is not already owned by the CWA/apprenticeship system.
