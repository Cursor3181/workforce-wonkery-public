The story

Chico has a strong education base. The bigger question is how much of the talent built here stays here.

Chico has a strong education base. The bigger question is how much of the talent built here stays here. Chico is growing more slowly than many places. The population is down 1.1% from the 2020 estimates base, 18.4% of residents are 65 or older, and unemployment was 6.2% in August 2026. Median rent is $1,414, and 60.0% of renters spend at least 30% of their income on housing.

Butte manufacturers share skilled-labor, supplier, training and technology-adoption needs. GMI already provides an employer network, while Butte College now has current advanced-manufacturing and engineering-support pathways with embedded work experience. Collective action adds value by connecting those assets to the same employer-defined technical jobs and progression outcomes.

Butte manufacturers share skilled-labor, supplier, training and technology-adoption needs. Worker value

What workers should gain

The local single-adult living-wage benchmark used in the canonical case is $24.06/hour. Industrial maintenance and production-supervision roles clear it, while machinist pay sits below it. Worker Value therefore depends on progression into higher-skill technical and supervisory careers, not manufacturing participation alone.

The move

Use current GMI subcommittees to select one or two shared technical job families where manufacturers will own work-based learning, hiring and progression outcomes.

Local operating story

The education system already includes local-company work experience.

Butte College's 2026–27 Advanced Manufacturing pathway includes ERP, robotic welding, 3D modeling, a 4-unit Internship with Local Trade Company and a 4-unit Advanced Internship with Local Trade Company. A newer Engineering Support Technician certificate also includes built-in work experience. The partnership's next proof point is whether manufacturers jointly convert that capacity into hires and advancement.

Evidence, risks & 90-day action plan

Backbone / host: Butte Grow Manufacturing Initiative + NoRTEC / Alliance for Workforce Development

90-day action

0–30 days: Name at least two manufacturer action owners and select the technical roles that recur across firms. 31–60 days: Map Butte College internship and technical capacity, AFWD/NoRTEC services and the wage ladder for those roles. 61–90 days: Align internships, customized training and WBL to verified employer demand; define internship-to-hire and wage-progression measures.

Year-one test

Employer-owned action teams; technical pathway participation; internship/WBL-to-hire conversion; wage progression; durable GMI operations.

Evidence gaps

Employer-owned action outcomes; formal worker or employee voice tied to GMI workforce decisions; internship-to-hire conversion; technical wage progression; recurring private-sector backbone support.

Stop / redesign if

Do not create another manufacturing table or judge success primarily by expos, networking events or training participation without employment and advancement outcomes.

What it means

Electrical contractors in Butte and Shasta Counties already share apprenticeship governance, technical training and workforce supply. The decision is how to make the mature system more responsive to electrification and infrastructure demand across two labor markets.

Worker value

What workers should gain

Electrician wages are above the local living-wage benchmarks in both Chico and Redding. The apprenticeship is paid, five years long and tuition-free, so Worker Value is already built into the pathway.

The move

Build one two-county electrical workload and apprentice-capacity view inside the existing JATC structure.

Local operating story

Access support can sit inside a mature apprenticeship system.

The training center serves the Sacramento and Shasta-Butte systems and offers a full-time earn-and-learn route. It also advertises up to $10,000 a year in childcare resources for approved participants through ERiCA. That is a useful example of solving an access barrier without weakening employer-labor control of apprenticeship.

Evidence, risks & 90-day action plan

Backbone / host: Shasta-Butte Electrical JATC / IBEW Local 340 + NECA employer partners

90-day action

0–30 days: Build a Chico-Redding electrical workload view tied to major infrastructure and electrification demand. 31–60 days: Compare projected demand with apprentice intake, worksite capacity and journey-level supply. 61–90 days: Have contractor and labor leaders choose the highest-priority throughput bottlenecks and assign action owners.

Year-one test

Contractor demand map; apprentice starts and OJT hours; completion and journey placement; durable labor-management operation.

Evidence gaps

Apprentice/worksite capacity; contractor demand; completion and journey placement; electrification project pipeline; recurring training resources.

Stop / redesign if

Do not create a duplicate construction partnership or extend electrical governance to unrelated trades. If no shared bottleneck exists, keep the current system and stop there.
