The story

The Solano CNA case is a job-quality strategy, not just a training program.

WDB Solano's CNA Upskilling work uses CUSP, a standardized employer-recognized ladder that adds micro-certifications, new titles and incremental wage increases as CNAs demonstrate higher levels of skill.

Employers pay education and registry fees and compensate CNAs for required training time. That creates a concrete collective-action test: can long-term-care facilities use a shared ladder to improve retention and wages, then connect workers to higher-level CNA, LVN or nursing and supervisory careers?

Local operating story

The career ladder travels with the worker.

CUSP records completed levels in a statewide registry, and participating employers agree to recognize prior CUSP achievement when hiring. The standardized ladder includes preceptor, restorative nursing, behavioral-health and dementia specialties. Solano should now measure how many CNAs advance, what wage gains they receive, whether turnover falls and how many continue into higher-wage roles.

Napa/Solano healthcare workforce

Suggested geography: Napa and Solano counties, spanning the Napa and Vallejo labor markets

What the targeted review found: HealthForce Partners is developing a localized Napa/Solano healthcare sector strategy with Aliados Health, the Napa-Solano Medical Society, and local healthcare and education partners. The Napa Worker Value test is now resolved through an advancement condition: entry/support occupations such as nursing assistants and medical assistants remain below Napa's $31.44/hour living-wage benchmark, while LVNs, clinical laboratory technologists/technicians, surgical technologists, and radiologic technologists are above it.

What is still missing: Current public materials do not identify two independent healthcare employers as action owners of one shared clinical-capacity, hiring, retention, advancement, or incumbent-worker action. The 2026 Queen of the Valley transition from Providence to NorthBay Health is important workforce-change context, but it is not proof of shared employer governance.

Reopen when: Two or more named independent Napa/Solano healthcare employers publicly co-own one shared workforce action. Use this one regional candidate rather than creating separate Napa and Solano partnership concepts.

Biomanufacturing & bioeconomy

Suggested geography: Solano County, centered on the Vacaville-Fairfield-Vallejo biomanufacturing and talent corridor

Why it remains visible: Solano has a substantial current talent ecosystem. Lonza reports more than 850 employees at its Vacaville biologics manufacturing site. Solano Community College operates industry-linked biotechnology and biomanufacturing pathways, and BioVille 2026 brought Lonza, Orca Bio and other biotech and supporting companies into a local career and talent event.

What the targeted review found: Employer density, training capacity and talent engagement are clear. A NIIMBL public-private project also brought UC Davis, Solano College, Cytiva and Lilly together to design industry-relevant cell-and-gene-therapy training. But career-fair participation, college hiring relationships, curriculum consultation and a training consortium do not establish two Solano employers jointly defining and owning one recurring workforce bottleneck.

Reopen when: Two or more named Solano/Vacaville biomanufacturing employers jointly own one certification, incumbent-worker, hiring, retention, advancement or other recurring workforce action.

Large-scale shipbuilding expansion & industrial base

Suggested geography: Solano County and Mare Island, with potential Bay/Delta sites and a broader Bay Area supplier network

What is already covered: Current maritime workforce coordination should run through SP-BAY-MARITIME-01. California Jobs First is funding a Bay Area maritime ecosystem backbone led by Solano EDC, and Cal Poly Maritime has new workforce and maritime-industrial capacity investment. Do not create a second workforce table for that activity.

What remains project-contingent: Large-scale shipbuilding expansion is still an industrial-development possibility rather than committed workforce demand. Mare Island Dry Dock closed, a major prospective Solano shipyard tenant selected Texas, and the proposed Solano Shipyard does not yet have a publicly committed operator.

Reopen a separate workforce case when: A named operator, committed shipbuilding or repair procurement/contract, committed site and infrastructure plan, or distinct multi-employer hiring demand emerges that the existing regional maritime partnership cannot own.
