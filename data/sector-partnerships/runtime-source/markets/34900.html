What workers should gain

The wage ladder is the decision signal. Entry and equipment roles shown here fall below the local living-wage benchmark, while agricultural management is far above it. The partnership should make the path between those levels more real and measurable.

The move

Turn NVG's workforce-focused survey and convening into a recurring employer-owned action portfolio centered on technical progression, succession and workforce resilience.

Local operating story

The industry is already asking the right workforce questions.

NVG's September 2026 member and industry survey is open to growers, vineyard managers, winery teams, suppliers and consultants because they share labor, market, regulatory, wildfire and next-generation pressures. ROOTSTOCK 2026 is also explicitly focused on the workforce that sustains Napa vineyards. The next move is to turn that listening into recurring actions with employer ownership and worker voice.

Backbone / host: Napa Valley Grapegrowers

90-day action

Year-one test

Employer-owned annual workforce agenda; pathway participation; technical/management advancement; worker/farmworker input; recurring dues/sponsor support covering defined backbone functions.

Evidence gaps

Formal worker/farmworker voice; measurable technical/management advancement; workforce-specific action ownership; recurring backbone coverage.

Stop / redesign if

Do not create another grower organization or a recruitment-only farm-labor initiative. Continue only if the workforce agenda produces measurable advancement or job-quality improvement.

The move

Use the existing Napa HIP to make advancement, retention and employee sustainability the core workforce agenda.

Local operating story

A mature employer table already exists. The outcome needs to change.

Napa HIP has operated since 2016 and is led by lodging and restaurant employers. Its action plan already includes workforce growth and housing/transportation policy. With tourism supporting about 16,000 local jobs, the next question is not whether employers need workers. It is whether the partnership improves retention, access and movement into better-paid roles.

Napa/Solano healthcare workforce

Suggested geography: Napa and Solano counties, spanning the Napa and Vallejo labor markets

What the targeted review found: HealthForce Partners is developing a localized Napa/Solano healthcare sector strategy with Aliados Health, the Napa-Solano Medical Society, and local healthcare and education partners. The Napa Worker Value test is now resolved through an advancement condition: entry/support occupations such as nursing assistants and medical assistants remain below Napa's $31.44/hour living-wage benchmark, while LVNs, clinical laboratory technologists/technicians, surgical technologists, and radiologic technologists are above it.

What is still missing: Current public materials do not identify two independent healthcare employers as action owners of one shared clinical-capacity, hiring, retention, advancement, or incumbent-worker action. The 2026 Queen of the Valley transition from Providence to NorthBay Health is important workforce-change context, but it is not proof of shared employer governance.

Reopen when: Two or more named independent Napa/Solano healthcare employers publicly co-own one shared workforce action. Use this one regional candidate rather than creating separate Napa and Solano partnership concepts.
