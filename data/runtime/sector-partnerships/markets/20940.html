The story

El Centro has very high unemployment at the same time that major new investment is arriving.

El Centro has very high unemployment at the same time that major new investment is arriving. The contrast is hard to miss. The population is up 0.9% from the 2020 estimates base, but unemployment was 21.9% in August 2026. A single adult needs about $22.37 an hour to cover basic costs, according to MIT.

Lithium, geothermal and related supply-chain projects create shared local-hiring, technical-training, apprenticeship and supplier-development needs that no single employer can solve efficiently. Imperial County already has one regional governance structure, a functioning technical-training platform, and an adopted Construction Workforce Ordinance. The construction question is therefore resolved: use the ordinance inside the existing Lithium Valley system rather than create another partnership. The remaining challenge is tying workforce capacity to real project schedules and employer hiring.

Worker value

What workers should gain

Imperial County's single-adult living-wage benchmark is $22.37/hour. Electrical and industrial-maintenance pathways clear it. The May 2026 Construction Workforce Ordinance adds prevailing-wage requirements, apprenticeship utilization, project workforce plans and a 40% local-worker goal for covered projects. The main worker-value risk is still training ahead of actual project demand.

The move

Translate each covered Lithium Valley project into an approved workforce plan, then connect trade demand, apprenticeship capacity, local hiring and LIFT technical pathways to verified project milestones.

Local operating story

Keep construction inside the Lithium Valley system.

Imperial County adopted the Lithium Valley Construction Workforce Ordinance on May 12, 2026. For covered projects, it requires an approved workforce plan, prevailing wage, apprenticeship utilization and a good-faith 40% local-worker goal. Those functions belong inside the existing Lithium Valley governance structure. A separate construction partnership would duplicate the County, labor and apprenticeship system rather than fill a missing coordination gap.
