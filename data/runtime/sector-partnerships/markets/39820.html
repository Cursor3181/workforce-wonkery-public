What it means

Use the partnership only where collective action adds value.

Teacher recruitment, substitute coverage, classified-to-teacher advancement and credential preparation span 24 districts. Shasta County already centralizes much of the infrastructure, which is exactly where collective action adds value. The next step is to make districts jointly own the priorities and outcomes rather than treating SCOE services alone as proof of employer governance.

Worker value

What workers should gain

Current SCOE evidence shows credentialed teacher pay starting above the local single-adult living-wage benchmark and progressing above $100,000. The strongest pathway is paid and job embedded: classified employees and paraprofessionals can remain employed while moving toward a bachelor's degree and teaching credential.

The move

Use Teach Shasta/SCOE to select two or three cross-district educator shortage priorities and attach measurable pathway actions to them.

Local operating story

Working school employees can advance without leaving the workforce.

Shasta College and Reach University allow associate-degree holders to transfer 100% of their credits into a job-embedded bachelor's pathway. Participants work for a regional education provider while completing the degree, and eligible classified employees can receive up to $4,272 per year in education support through June 2028. That makes advancement a workforce strategy rather than a request to leave work and return to school.

Evidence, risks & 90-day action plan

Backbone / host: Shasta County Office of Education / Teach Shasta

90-day action

0–30 days: Name at least two district action leads and identify the shortage fields or educator roles that genuinely require countywide coordination. 31–60 days: Map classified, paraprofessional and substitute progression into degree/credential pathways, including financial support, mentor/worksite capacity and salary progression. 61–90 days: Have districts co-own completion-to-hire and retention measures; add worker/labor feedback on job quality and barriers to advancement.

Year-one test

District-owned annual agenda; pathway completions and hires; substitute-to-credential conversions; one-year retention; recurring support for core pathway/backbone functions.

Evidence gaps

Two independent district action owners; classified-to-teacher completion/hire outcomes; substitute-to-credential progression; one-year retention; recurring district/pathway support beyond current grants.

Stop / redesign if

Do not create another educator intermediary. If districts do not co-own shared priorities, keep SCOE services as shared infrastructure rather than label the system fully employer-led.
