What it means

Use the partnership only where collective action adds value.

Manufacturers already share technician and automation needs across county lines, local sector partnerships exist, and Central Valley FAME provides a concrete multi-employer paid pathway. Regional coordination adds value only where job families and competencies are genuinely shared.

Worker value

What workers should gain

The paid work-study model is a strong access strategy, but the launch wage sat below the local living-wage benchmark. Worker Value should therefore be demonstrated through completion, technical placement and post-completion wage progression.

The move

Connect local employer tables around a small set of common technician job families and use FAME and other paid pathways as the shared implementation layer.

Local operating story

The regional technical pathway is already operating.

Central Valley FAME became California's first FAME chapter and enrolled its first students in fall 2025. Students work for sponsoring manufacturers three days per week and attend technical training two days per week. The next step is to measure outcomes and connect the pathway to the few job families that truly justify four-county coordination.

Evidence, risks & 90-day action plan

Backbone / host: Tulare WIB regional lead + Fresno EDC/FAME + local industry sector partnerships

90-day action

0–30 days: Map existing local tables and identify the technician, automation and maintenance roles that recur regionally. 31–60 days: Name cross-county employer champions and establish a common competency map without replacing local governance. 61–90 days: Tie FAME and other paid pathways to employer sponsorship, verified worksite capacity and post-completion wage targets.

Year-one test

Common competency map; sponsored paid pathways; completion/placement/wage measures; recurring regional coordination support.

Evidence gaps

Common regional job families; formal participant, worker, or worker-representative voice in sector governance; FAME completion, placement and wage outcomes; post-grant regional financing.

Stop / redesign if

Do not replace functioning local sector partnerships with a new regional table, and do not scale training based only on grant capacity.

The story

The regional training platform exists. Employer ownership is the next test.

F3 AgTEC already connects farmworkers, industry partners and seven Central Valley community colleges through a 12-unit competency-based Ag Systems Certificate. The program is free, available in English or Spanish, and built around practical skills such as equipment operation, troubleshooting, digital literacy and food safety.

The partnership question is no longer whether training can be offered. It is whether employers in each market will jointly define technical roles, sponsor work-based learning and measure whether workers move into better-paid technical, equipment and supervisory jobs.
