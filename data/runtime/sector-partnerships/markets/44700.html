The move

Local operating story

This partnership can point to outcomes, not only activity.

HealthForce's 2026 reporting combines worker and employer results. HOPE participants move into RN licensure and remain with local employers, while the behavioral-health partnership uses paid internships, scholarships, certification support, loan repayment and retention bonuses. HealthForce reports a county clinician vacancy rate falling from 24% three years earlier to under 3%.

Advanced manufacturing

Suggested geography: North San Joaquin Valley across Merced, Stanislaus and San Joaquin counties; SP-STAN-MFG-01 remains the current canonical employer-owned anchor in Modesto

What the fresh review found: The regional pathway system is more concrete than the earlier discovery card showed. Modesto Junior College now names multiple member employers in the Stanislaus County Manufacturing & Maintenance Joint Apprenticeship Committee, where employers hire apprentices for maintenance, electro-mechanical, machining, packaging, calibration and instrumentation roles. MCCV's September 2026 pathways page also documents ongoing employer-paid manufacturing apprenticeships in Merced and San Joaquin.

Scope decision: Do not create a second regional manufacturing partnership. The Stanislaus joint apprenticeship and MCCV/AMP strengthen the existing SP-STAN-MFG-01 system. The Merced and San Joaquin pathways are valuable regional infrastructure, but current public evidence does not show employers across counties jointly owning one shared technical workforce action.

Reopen regional mapping when: Employers spanning Merced and/or San Joaquin with Stanislaus jointly own the same technical occupation, apprenticeship, paid work-based learning, hiring, retention or advancement action.

Zero-emission mobility & clean transportation

Suggested geography: San Joaquin County / Stockton-Lodi, using existing regional clean-transportation and technical-training systems

What the targeted review found: The employer ecosystem is real. The Port of Stockton, San Joaquin Regional Transit District and Míocar all participated in the SJCOG workforce study, while Green Economy Lab and Delta College provide technical-training capacity. However, the study found limited direct labor demand for the original narrow e-bike, carshare and EV-charger occupations.

Workforce response: Integrate electrification skills into broader automotive, electrical/electronic, industrial-maintenance, transportation/logistics and construction pathways instead of building a separate sector partnership around occupations without verified demand.

Reopen when: Two or more named employers or infrastructure operators jointly own the same technical pathway, hiring, incumbent-worker, retention or advancement action tied to funded deployment.

Circular bioeconomy & biomanufacturing

Suggested geography: North San Joaquin Valley, with Modesto/Stanislaus as the current manufacturing and Innovation Campus anchor and Merced/San Joaquin linked through the regional ecosystem

Why it remains visible: Four named manufacturers are being recruited into Stanislaus County through $2 million in BEAM Circular awards, the region has more than $1 million in new bioeconomy workforce projects, and shared Innovation Campus infrastructure is coming online. BEAM's Good Jobs Framework adds an explicit $32/hour family-sustaining-wage target for industry development.

What the targeted review found: Central Valley Circular, Artefact, Mango Materials and Full Circle Brewing remain separate employer-attraction or expansion projects, and the awards are contingent on project milestones. Current evidence strengthens employer density, scale and job-quality intent, but it still does not identify one shared occupation or skill bottleneck jointly owned by two employers.

Reopen when: At least two employers identify the same occupations or skill gap or jointly own a hiring, training, retention or advancement action. Until then, use the existing BEAM ecosystem to convene first rather than treating it as a scored workforce partnership.
